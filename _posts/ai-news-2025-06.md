June was less about new capability than new economics: frontier reasoning got dramatically cheaper, the talent market went vertical, and a serious agent became free for anyone with a terminal.

## OpenAI cuts o3 by 80% and launches o3-pro (June 10)

OpenAI repriced its flagship reasoning model overnight: o3's API price dropped 80%, to $2 per million input tokens and $8 per million output. The same day brought o3-pro, a slower, pricier tier built to think longer for higher reliability. The pairing made the strategy explicit - commoditize the base model, sell the premium version to those who cannot afford a wrong answer.

## Meta bets big on Scale AI, and takes its founder (June 12)

Scale AI announced a major new investment from Meta - reported at roughly $14.3 billion for a 49% stake - and that its founder, Alexandr Wang, would join Meta to work on its superintelligence effort. Scale remained independent. The deal showed where the frontier race was heading: not just chips and models, but the data-labeling pipelines and the people who built them.

## Google open-sources the Gemini CLI (June 25)

Google released Gemini CLI, an open-source agent that brings Gemini 2.5 Pro into the terminal under an Apache 2.0 license. A personal Google account gets a generous free tier - 60 requests a minute, 1,000 a day - and the tool speaks MCP, so it plugs into the wider agent ecosystem. The command line, the oldest interface in computing, became one of the busiest fronts in the agent wars.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

Cheaper reasoning changes the math on extraction: jobs that were too expensive to run across thousands of pages became routine in June. When the model costs less, the bottleneck moves to data quality - the pages, not the prompts. That is where NextRows earns its keep, turning whole sites into validated tables at whatever scale the new prices allow.
