February made models bigger in memory, richer in output and easier to run yourself. A million-token window, minute-long generated video and a new open model family all arrived within one week.

## Google introduces Gemini 1.5 (February 15)

Google introduced Gemini 1.5, led by Gemini 1.5 Pro. Google said 1.5 Pro reaches quality comparable to 1.0 Ultra while using less compute, and that it can run up to 1 million tokens consistently, the longest context window of any large-scale foundation model at that point. The long context was offered as a limited preview to developers and enterprise customers. A window that size means whole documents, codebases or long videos can go into a single prompt.

## OpenAI previews Sora (February 15)

OpenAI announced Sora, a model that generates video from text prompts. Reuters reported that it can produce minute-long videos, and that access was limited to red teamers testing for flaws and to visual artists, designers and filmmakers giving feedback. It was a preview, not a product launch.

## Google releases Gemma open models (February 21)

Google released Gemma, a family of lightweight open models built from the same research and technology as Gemini. Weights came in two sizes, Gemma 2B and Gemma 7B, each with pre-trained and instruction-tuned variants, along with a Responsible Generative AI Toolkit and toolchains for inference and fine-tuning.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

Longer context changes what is possible for data extraction. When a model can read a long report or a stack of pages in one pass, the hard part shifts from fitting the input to getting a clean, consistent table out.
