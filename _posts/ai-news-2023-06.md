June sketched the two halves of the AI stack: models that can act on software, and rules that govern the acting. Both halves were drawn in the same week.

## OpenAI teaches its models to call tools (June 13)

An API update added function calling to GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 Turbo: a developer describes a function, and the model decides when to call it and what arguments to pass. The release also brought a 16,000-token version of GPT-3.5 Turbo and price cuts on embeddings and input tokens. Chatbots that can reliably reach into other software are the foundation everything agent-shaped was later built on.

## The European Parliament adopts its AI Act position (June 14)

MEPs voted 499 to 28, with 93 abstentions, to adopt Parliament's negotiating position on the AI Act. The draft banned practices like real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces and added new obligations for foundation models. The vote set up the final round of negotiations with EU governments - the first serious attempt anywhere to write general rules for AI.

## Meta shows Voicebox (June 16)

Meta introduced Voicebox, a generative speech model that generalizes across tasks it was never explicitly trained for, including cloning a voice from a two-second sample and editing audio by prompt. Citing the risk of misuse, Meta published the research and audio samples but not the model itself - a preview of the release debates to come.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

Models that call tools need those tools fed with well-structured data, and the coming rules ask you to know where that data came from. NextRows works at that layer: plain-language requests in, validated tables out.
