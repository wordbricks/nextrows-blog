October answered a question the model releases had left open: where does AI actually live? The answers - inside your apps, inside your browser, and inside a newly restructured company.

## DevDay: apps and AgentKit come to ChatGPT (October 6)

At DevDay, OpenAI turned ChatGPT into a platform twice over. Apps in ChatGPT, built on a new Apps SDK atop the open Model Context Protocol, let services like Spotify, Canva, Coursera, Figma and Zillow run inside the conversation. For developers, AgentKit added Agent Builder - a visual canvas for multi-agent workflows - plus ChatKit for embedding chat and expanded Evals for measuring agents. The chatbot became a distribution channel.

## ChatGPT Atlas: a browser with AI built in (October 21)

Two weeks later OpenAI launched ChatGPT Atlas, a Mac browser with ChatGPT in every tab. It can answer questions about the page you are on, remember context from sites you visit if you let it, and - for Plus, Pro and Business users - run in agent mode, researching and acting across tabs on your behalf. The browser, software's oldest surface, got a copilot by default.

## OpenAI and Microsoft sign their next chapter (October 28)

OpenAI and Microsoft signed a definitive agreement completing OpenAI's recapitalization. The nonprofit became the OpenAI Foundation, which controls a for-profit public benefit corporation; Microsoft took a stake valued at roughly $135 billion - about 27% - and OpenAI committed to purchasing an additional $250 billion of Azure services, with Microsoft's IP rights extended through 2032. The industry's defining partnership got its long-term footing.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

October moved AI onto the surfaces where the web is consumed. Apps, agent builders and browsers all act on live pages - booking, buying, comparing, extracting. Each of those actions works best when the underlying data is structured rather than scraped on the fly.
