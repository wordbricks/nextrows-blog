March covered all three layers of the AI stack: the model, the law around it, and the hardware under it.

## Anthropic launches the Claude 3 family (March 4)

Anthropic announced Claude 3 in three sizes: Haiku, Sonnet and Opus, in ascending order of capability. Opus and Sonnet became available in claude.ai and the Claude API, which was generally available in 159 countries, with Haiku to follow. Anthropic said Opus outperformed its peers on common benchmarks including MMLU and GPQA, and pointed to live customer chats, auto-completions and data extraction as fast-response uses for the smaller models.

## European Parliament adopts the AI Act (March 13)

The European Parliament approved the Artificial Intelligence Act with 523 votes in favour, 46 against and 49 abstentions. The regulation sets obligations by risk level, bans applications such as social scoring and untargeted scraping of facial images for recognition databases, and adds safeguards for general purpose AI. It had been agreed with member states in December 2023.

## NVIDIA announces the Blackwell platform (March 18)

At GTC, NVIDIA announced the Blackwell platform, built to run real-time generative AI on trillion-parameter language models at up to 25x less cost and energy consumption than its predecessor. NVIDIA listed Amazon Web Services, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Oracle, Tesla and xAI among the organizations expected to adopt it.
