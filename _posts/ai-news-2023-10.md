October widened the map: a Chinese lab claimed parity with the frontier, the cloud bidding war escalated, and the US government wrote its first broad rules for the technology.

## Baidu claims Ernie 4.0 matches GPT-4 (October 17)

At its Baidu World event in Beijing, CEO Robin Li unveiled Ernie 4.0 and said it matches GPT-4 across understanding, generation, reasoning and memory. Whatever the benchmark debate, it was the strongest public claim yet from a Chinese lab in the foundation model race, and a reminder that the frontier was not a one-country story.

## Google agrees to invest up to $2 billion in Anthropic (October 27)

The Wall Street Journal reported that Google had agreed to invest up to $2 billion in Anthropic - $500 million up front and $1.5 billion over time - weeks after Amazon's larger commitment to the same company. The contest for the leading independent AI lab had become a bidding war between cloud providers.

## Biden signs a sweeping AI executive order (October 30)

President Biden signed an executive order requiring developers of the most powerful AI systems to share safety test results with the US government, directing NIST to set standards for red-team testing, and addressing AI's effects on privacy, civil rights and workers. It was the most extensive government action on AI anywhere to that point, and it arrived a day before the UK's global AI Safety Summit.
