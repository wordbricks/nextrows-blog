January was about packaging. Custom assistants got a storefront, a premium subscription reached everyday office software, and a research system showed how far AI reasoning had come in geometry.

## OpenAI opens the GPT Store (January 10)

OpenAI began rolling out the GPT Store to ChatGPT Plus, Team and Enterprise users. Two months after GPTs were announced, OpenAI said users had already created more than 3 million custom versions of ChatGPT. The store added a community leaderboard with categories such as writing, research, programming and education, and featured GPTs from AllTrails, Consensus, Khan Academy and Canva. Building a GPT required no code, so the store turned prompt writing into a distribution channel.

## Microsoft launches Copilot Pro (January 15)

Microsoft introduced Copilot Pro, a premium subscription for individuals. It brings Copilot into Microsoft 365 Personal and Family apps and lets subscribers create their own Copilot GPTs. The same announcement made the Copilot app generally available on iOS and Android and cited more than 5 billion chats to date. The Verge reported the price as $20 per month.

## Google DeepMind publishes AlphaGeometry in Nature (January 17)

In a paper in Nature, Google DeepMind described AlphaGeometry, which solved 25 of 30 Olympiad geometry problems within the standard time limit. The previous best system solved 10, and the average human gold medalist solves 25.9 on the same set. The system pairs a neural language model with a rule-based deduction engine, and it was trained on synthetic data instead of human demonstrations.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

January's common thread: AI moved from demo to product. Storefronts, subscriptions and bundled office features all depend on assistants reading and producing structured information reliably. That is the layer NextRows works in - you describe the data you want in natural language, and schema validation makes sure the output is the table you asked for.
