February was the month the frontier labs stopped taking turns - two flagship agent releases landed on the same day - and the open-weight camp proved it could compete on coding at a fraction of the price.

## Anthropic ships Opus 4.6 with agent teams (February 5)

Claude Opus 4.6 arrived with a feature that previewed the rest of the year: agent teams in Claude Code, which split a large task across multiple agents that coordinate in parallel. Anthropic also released Claude in PowerPoint as a research preview, upgraded Claude in Excel, and held pricing at $5/$25 per million tokens. TechCrunch reported a 1 million token context window, matching Sonnet.

## OpenAI answers the same day with GPT-5.3-Codex (February 5)

Later that same day, OpenAI introduced GPT-5.3-Codex - "our first model that was instrumental in creating itself," in OpenAI's own words: the Codex team used early versions to debug its own training and manage its own deployment. OpenAI said it set new industry highs on SWE-Bench Pro and Terminal-Bench. Both labs now treat the coding agent as the flagship product, and both shipped on February 5 to prove it.

## MiniMax M2.5 undercuts everyone (February 12)

A week later, MiniMax released M2.5, an open-weights model it called state of the art in coding, agentic tool use and office work - posting an OpenCode score of 76.1 against Opus 4.6's 75.9, and explicitly designing for agents "without having to worry about cost." The Decoder called the pitch "intelligence too cheap to meter." The era of the expensive extraction pipeline was ending.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

Two curves crossed in February: agents got better at long, multi-step work, and the cost of running them fell hard. High-volume data jobs - the kind that read hundreds of pages into tables - became dramatically cheaper to run well.
