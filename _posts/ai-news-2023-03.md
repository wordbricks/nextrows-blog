![Timeline of March 2023 AI news](/assets/blog/ai-news-2023-03-timeline.svg)

March gave the industry its new benchmark model and, a week later, its loudest call to slow down. Both moved the conversation; only one moved the product.

## OpenAI launches GPT-4 (March 14)

GPT-4 arrived as OpenAI's most capable system: it accepts images as well as text as input, and OpenAI said it scored around the top 10 percent of test-takers on a simulated bar exam, where GPT-3.5 had landed near the bottom 10 percent. It went live for ChatGPT Plus subscribers and through an API waitlist the same day, with Microsoft's new Bing quietly confirmed to have been running on it all along.

## Google opens Bard to early users (March 21)

After six weeks of testing, Google opened access to Bard in the US and UK through a waitlist. The company was careful to call it an early experiment that complements search rather than replacing it - a notable hedge from the company that invented the technology underneath it all.

## An open letter calls for a pause (March 22)

The Future of Life Institute published an open letter asking all AI labs to pause training of systems more powerful than GPT-4 for at least six months. Early signatories included Elon Musk and Steve Wozniak, and the count passed a thousand names within days. No lab paused, but the question of pace moved from mailing lists to front pages, where it stayed for the rest of the year.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

March made two things clear: model capability was compounding, and nobody agreed on the rules. Whatever the rules turn out to be, they will run on verifiable data - the kind NextRows extracts from the messy web into validated tables.
