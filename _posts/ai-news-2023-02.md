Two months after ChatGPT reset expectations for what a search box could do, the two biggest search companies answered within twenty-four hours of each other, and Meta opened a third front from the research side.

## Google announces Bard (February 6)

Sundar Pichai introduced Bard, an experimental conversational AI service powered by Google's LaMDA model, opening first to trusted testers with wider availability promised in the coming weeks. The announcement framed Bard as a way to combine the breadth of the world's knowledge with the fluency of a large language model - and it landed while Google was widely seen as playing catch-up.

## Microsoft puts an AI copilot in Bing and Edge (February 7)

A day later, at an event in Redmond, Microsoft launched a new Bing running on a next-generation OpenAI model it called Prometheus, alongside an Edge browser with an AI copilot for the web. Microsoft pitched the pair as an answer engine for complex questions rather than a list of blue links, and opened a waitlist that millions joined within days.

## Meta releases LLaMA to researchers (February 24)

Meta introduced LLaMA, a family of foundation language models ranging from 7 billion to 65 billion parameters, trained on publicly available data and released to researchers under a noncommercial license. The release put serious model capability in the hands of anyone cleared to download it.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

Search spent February turning into an answer engine, and answers are only as good as the data underneath. For teams that need the underlying facts as clean tables rather than prose, that extraction step is what NextRows automates.
