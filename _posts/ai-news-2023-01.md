The first month of 2023 produced no headline model. It produced the deal flow: generative AI became a line item, a content strategy, and a moderation problem, all within four weeks.

## Microsoft deepens its OpenAI partnership (January 23)

Microsoft announced what it called the third phase of its partnership with OpenAI: a multiyear, multibillion-dollar investment meant to accelerate AI breakthroughs and bring them to customers at scale. Microsoft did not name a figure; press reports put it near $10 billion. Coming weeks after ChatGPT's explosive debut, the deal confirmed that the AI race would be run by whoever could fund the compute.

## BuzzFeed says AI will help write its content (January 26)

The Wall Street Journal reported an internal BuzzFeed memo in which CEO Jonah Peretti told staff that AI would move from a research project into the core of the business, starting with quizzes and personalized content built on OpenAI's tools. BuzzFeed's stock soared on the news. A struggling media company had become the first test case for AI-assisted content as a business plan.

## OpenAI ships a classifier for AI-written text (January 31)

OpenAI released a free tool trained to tell AI-written text from human writing, pitched to teachers facing a semester of chatbot-written essays. OpenAI itself called the classifier "not fully reliable": in its own evaluation it correctly flagged only 26 percent of AI-written text. Even the lab that generated the text could not reliably detect it.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

January's pattern: as more of the web is written by machines, knowing what you are reading becomes a business question. That is the layer NextRows works in - you describe the data you want in natural language, and schema validation keeps the extracted output in the shape you asked for.
