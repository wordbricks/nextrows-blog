September introduced models that spend time reasoning before they reply, a family of open models for phones, and a high-profile decision on AI regulation in California.

## OpenAI releases o1-preview and o1-mini (September 12)

OpenAI released o1, a family of reasoning models. o1-preview and o1-mini became available to ChatGPT Plus and Team users and through the API. TechCrunch reported tight limits at launch, 30 messages per week for o1-preview and 50 for o1-mini, and API pricing for o1-preview of $15 per million input tokens and $60 per million output tokens, six times the cost of GPT-4o.

## Meta releases Llama 3.2 (September 25)

Meta released Llama 3.2 with vision models at 11B and 90B parameters and lightweight text models at 1B and 3B designed for edge and mobile devices. The 1B and 3B models support a 128K token context length and were enabled on day one for Qualcomm and MediaTek hardware.

## California Governor Newsom vetoes SB 1047 (September 29)

California Governor Gavin Newsom vetoed SB 1047, a bill that would have created first-in-the-nation safety measures for large AI models, AP reported. The veto message is published on the governor's site.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

Reasoning models trade speed and cost for accuracy on hard problems, which is a different tool from fast extraction. For web data work the practical lesson is to match the model to the job and validate the output either way. NextRows keeps that validation step in the loop.
