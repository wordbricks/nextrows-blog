The first month of 2026 did not bring one dazzling model. It brought plumbing: a transaction standard, a no-code agent tool, and a compute deal aimed at real-time agents.

## Google launches the Universal Commerce Protocol (January 11)

At the National Retail Federation, Google introduced the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP), an open-source standard for agentic commerce. In Google's own words, UCP establishes "a common language" so AI agents can handle commerce journeys between consumer surfaces and businesses. It was developed with Shopify, Etsy, Wayfair, Target and Walmart, and endorsed by more than 20 partners including Mastercard, Stripe and Visa. The message to merchants: make your inventory machine-readable, because the next shopper may be an agent.

## Anthropic brings Cowork to the Claude desktop app (January 12)

A day later, Anthropic launched Cowork - widely described as "Claude Code without the code" (TechCrunch). It gives non-technical users the agentic workflow of Claude Code for everyday tasks: point Claude at files and let it plan and execute multi-step work. The low-code market's newest entrant was not a forms builder; it was an agent.

## OpenAI signs a $10 billion compute deal with Cerebras (January 14)

OpenAI announced a partnership with Cerebras to add 750 megawatts of "ultra low-latency" AI compute to its platform, integrated into the inference stack in phases. Reuters and the Wall Street Journal put the deal's value at around $10 billion. OpenAI's own post explains the logic: agents that browse, code and act need faster responses, and Cerebras' wafer-scale chips exist to cut inference latency.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

January's common thread: the web is being rebuilt for two audiences, humans and machines. When checkout becomes a protocol and office work becomes an agent task, clean structured data stops being a nice-to-have.
