October brought the field's highest formal recognition and a new kind of agent: a model that operates a computer the way a person does.

## Nobel Prize in Physics goes to Hopfield and Hinton (October 8)

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences awarded the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics to John J. Hopfield and Geoffrey Hinton for foundational discoveries and inventions that enable machine learning with artificial neural networks.

## Nobel Prize in Chemistry recognizes protein AI (October 9)

The 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry went one half to David Baker for computational protein design, and the other half jointly to Demis Hassabis and John Jumper of Google DeepMind for protein structure prediction. The Academy described their AI model as solving a 50-year-old problem.

## Anthropic launches computer use in public beta (October 22)

Anthropic announced an upgraded Claude 3.5 Sonnet, a new Claude 3.5 Haiku, and computer use in public beta on the API. Developers can direct Claude to look at a screen, move a cursor, click buttons and type text. Anthropic called Claude 3.5 Sonnet the first frontier AI model to offer computer use in public beta, and said the capability was still experimental, at times cumbersome and error-prone.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

Computer use points at the next question for web data: agents that browse and click will need structured results, not screenshots of pages. NextRows takes a plain-language request and returns the data as a validated table.
