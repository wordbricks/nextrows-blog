In May, assistants learned to listen, look and answer at conversation speed. Governments and labs also agreed on a shared set of voluntary safety commitments.

## OpenAI launches GPT-4o (May 13)

OpenAI announced GPT-4o, a flagship model that accepts any combination of text, audio, image and video as input and generates text, audio and image outputs. It can respond to audio in as little as 232 milliseconds, 320 milliseconds on average, close to human conversational response time. It matched GPT-4 Turbo on English text and code, and was faster and 50% cheaper in the API.

## Google unveils Gemini 1.5 Flash and Project Astra (May 14)

At Google I/O, Google introduced Gemini 1.5 Flash, a lighter model built for speed and efficiency, and Project Astra, its vision for future AI assistants. Both 1.5 Pro and 1.5 Flash were available in public preview with a 1 million token window, and 1.5 Pro with a 2 million token window was offered through a waitlist.

## AI Seoul Summit secures Frontier AI Safety Commitments (May 21)

The UK and Republic of Korea governments announced that 16 organizations had agreed to the Frontier AI Safety Commitments at the AI Seoul Summit. They included Amazon, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Samsung Electronics, Naver and xAI. The commitments are voluntary. Signatories pledged to publish a safety framework focused on severe risks by the next AI summit, in France.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

Multimodal input matters for data work. Invoices, screenshots, scanned tables and web pages are not clean text, and a model that reads them natively makes more of the world extractable. The remaining job is turning that reading into structured, validated output, which is where NextRows sits.
