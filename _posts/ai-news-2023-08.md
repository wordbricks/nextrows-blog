August put numbers and permits on the AI boom: who pays for it, who profits from it, and who gets to deploy it in public.

## San Francisco approves paid robotaxis around the clock (August 10)

California regulators approved permits letting Cruise and Waymo charge fares for driverless rides across San Francisco at all hours, making it the first major US city with two competing commercial robotaxi fleets. The decision, over objections about blocked emergency vehicles and surprise traffic jams, moved autonomous driving from pilot project to city policy fight.

## Nvidia's revenue doubles on AI chip demand (August 23)

Nvidia reported second-quarter revenue of $13.51 billion, up 101 percent from a year earlier, with $10.32 billion from data center chips, and forecast roughly $16 billion for the following quarter. If the AI boom had a scoreboard, this was it: the picks-and-shovels supplier was growing faster than any of its customers.

## OpenAI launches ChatGPT Enterprise (August 28)

ChatGPT Enterprise added business-grade guarantees - SOC 2 compliance, no training on company data, unlimited GPT-4 access - for OpenAI's fastest-growing use case. OpenAI said teams at more than 80 percent of Fortune 500 companies had already adopted ChatGPT in some form. The consumer phenomenon officially became enterprise software.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

August put prices on the AI buildout: chips, permits, enterprise seats. The less visible cost is getting raw web data into a shape models can trust, and that is the part NextRows exists to shrink.
