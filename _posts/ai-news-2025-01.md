The first month of 2025 reset two assumptions in a single week: what a frontier model costs to build, and what building AI at national scale requires. A third announcement pointed at where all of it is heading - software that acts on its own.

## DeepSeek releases R1, an open reasoning model (January 20)

DeepSeek, a small Chinese lab, released DeepSeek-R1: a reasoning model that matches OpenAI's o1 on math and coding benchmarks, published as open weights under an MIT license with a detailed training paper. The recipe leaned on large-scale reinforcement learning rather than expensive human labeling. Within days the chat app topped app store charts, and a week later the release rippled through public markets, forcing investors to reprice how much compute frontier AI really demands.

## OpenAI, SoftBank and Oracle announce the $500 billion Stargate Project (January 21)

At the White House, OpenAI, SoftBank, Oracle and MGX announced the Stargate Project: an intention to invest $500 billion over four years in US AI infrastructure, with $100 billion deployed immediately and construction already underway in Texas. Arm, Microsoft and NVIDIA joined as key technology partners. Whatever else 2025 would bring, January 21 made one thing plain: it would be a year measured in gigawatts.

## OpenAI's Operator learns to drive a browser (January 23)

Two days later OpenAI launched Operator, a research preview for US Pro subscribers. Operator controls its own browser window - clicking, typing and scrolling through ordinary websites the way a person would - to fill out forms, order groceries or book a table. It runs on the Computer-Using Agent model, which pairs GPT-4o's vision with reasoning, and it asks for confirmation before consequential steps. The direction was set: the web would soon have a second kind of user.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

January's three announcements share one dependency: machines that read and act on the web need pages they can parse. A reasoning model, a data center and a browsing agent all assume clean, structured information underneath. That is the layer NextRows works in - you describe the data you want in natural language, and schema validation makes sure the output is the table you asked for.
