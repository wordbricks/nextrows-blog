April was release season. In eleven days, Meta, then OpenAI twice, shipped the models that would define the middle of the year - and the strongest of them were built to be taken apart, extended and aimed at real work.

## Meta releases the Llama 4 herd (April 5)

Meta dropped the first Llama 4 models: Scout and Maverick, a pair of natively multimodal, mixture-of-experts models released with open weights. Scout fits on a single high-end GPU yet carries a ten-million-token context window; Maverick, with 400 billion total parameters, beats GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 Flash on many benchmarks. Both are distilled from Behemoth, a two-trillion-parameter teacher still in training.

## OpenAI ships GPT-4.1 in the API (April 14)

GPT-4.1 arrived API-only, in standard, mini and nano sizes, with a one-million-token context window and large gains in coding and instruction following. It was a workhorse release, not a chatbot one: aimed squarely at developers building agents and long-document pipelines, and a reminder that the API and the app were now different products with different needs.

## o3 and o4-mini learn to use tools while reasoning (April 16)

OpenAI's newest reasoning models, o3 and o4-mini, became the first to wield the full ChatGPT toolset - web search, Python, image analysis - inside their chain of thought, deciding mid-reasoning when and how to look things up. o4-mini in particular posted startling math scores for its size and price. Reasoning stopped being a monologue and started looking like research.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

April's models all point the same way: more context, more tools, more autonomy - all of it hungry for structured input. A million-token window is only as good as the data you pour into it, and a model that fetches mid-thought needs sources worth fetching.
