September was about scale in every direction: the largest infrastructure commitment in tech history, a coding model that works for thirty hours straight, and video generation with a feed attached.

## NVIDIA plans up to $100 billion for OpenAI's compute (September 22)

OpenAI and NVIDIA announced a letter of intent for a landmark partnership: at least 10 gigawatts of NVIDIA systems for OpenAI's next-generation infrastructure, with NVIDIA intending to invest up to $100 billion in OpenAI progressively as each gigawatt is deployed. The first gigawatt is targeted for the second half of 2026 on NVIDIA's Vera Rubin platform. Compute, capital and chips fused into a single deal.

## Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 4.5 tops the coding charts (September 29)

Anthropic introduced Claude Sonnet 4.5, which it calls the best coding model in the world. It leads SWE-bench Verified, more than doubles its predecessor on real computer-use tasks, and - most strikingly - can maintain focus on a complex, multi-step task for more than 30 hours. Agents stopped being measured in prompts and started being measured in shifts.

## Sora 2 makes video generation social (September 30)

OpenAI released Sora 2, a video and audio generation model with markedly better physics and controllability, plus synchronized dialogue and sound. It arrived with its own invite-based iOS app and a feature called cameos: verify your likeness once, and you or your friends can appear in generated scenes. Within a day, Sora was the app everyone was trying to get into.
