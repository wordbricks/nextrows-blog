April's news was about where AI runs: in an open model anyone can download, in a model small enough for a phone, and in a humanoid robot built for work.

## Boston Dynamics unveils a fully electric Atlas (April 17)

Boston Dynamics announced the retirement of its hydraulic Atlas and unveiled a fully electric Atlas robot designed for real-world applications. TechCrunch reported the reveal came a day after the hydraulic model was retired, and that the company framed the new robot around commercial humanoid work.

## Meta releases Llama 3 (April 18)

Meta introduced Llama 3 with pretrained and instruction-tuned models at 8B and 70B parameters. Meta said the models would be available on AWS, Databricks, Google Cloud, Hugging Face, Microsoft Azure, NVIDIA NIM, Snowflake and others, and released new safety tools including Llama Guard 2 and Code Shield. Meta also said larger models, longer context windows and more capabilities would follow.

## Microsoft introduces the Phi-3 small models (April 23)

Microsoft introduced Phi-3, a family of open small language models. Phi-3-mini, at 3.8B parameters, became available on Azure AI Studio, Hugging Face and Ollama, in 4K and 128K token context variants. Microsoft said it was the first model in its class to support a 128K context window with little impact on quality.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

Open and small models mean extraction can run closer to the data, on cheaper hardware, with more control. The question stops being whether a model can read a page and becomes whether the result is consistent across thousands of pages. That is what schema-guided extraction in NextRows is built for.
