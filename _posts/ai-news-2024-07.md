July pushed on cost, openness and reasoning at once. A cheap small model, a frontier-scale open model and a math result made the month hard to summarize with one headline.

## OpenAI launches GPT-4o mini (July 18)

OpenAI announced GPT-4o mini, its most cost-efficient small model. It scored 82% on MMLU and was priced at 15 cents per million input tokens and 60 cents per million output tokens, more than 60% cheaper than GPT-3.5 Turbo. At launch it supported text and vision in the API.

## Meta releases Llama 3.1 with a 405B model (July 23)

Meta released Llama 3.1, expanding context length to 128K, adding support for eight languages and including Llama 3.1 405B, which Meta described as the first frontier-level open source AI model. Meta also published a letter from Mark Zuckerberg arguing for open source AI, and opened a request for comment on the Llama Stack API.

## DeepMind's AlphaProof reaches IMO silver-medal level (July 25)

Google DeepMind said AlphaProof, a reinforcement-learning system for formal math reasoning, and AlphaGeometry 2 together solved four of the six problems at the International Mathematical Olympiad. That matched the level of a silver medalist, a first for an AI system.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

Cheap and open models change the economics of extraction. Pulling data from thousands of pages becomes affordable, and the choice of model becomes a cost and control decision. NextRows lets you describe the output you want and check it against a schema, whichever model does the reading.
