November compressed a model cycle into twelve days. OpenAI, Google and Anthropic each shipped a flagship, and each claimed - with benchmarks in hand - to be the one at the frontier.

## OpenAI ships GPT-5.1 (November 12)

GPT-5.1 upgraded both halves of the GPT-5 system. Instant became warmer and better at following instructions; Thinking learned to adapt its reasoning time - spending more on hard problems, less on easy ones. OpenAI also leaned into personality, adding preset tones so people can shape how ChatGPT sounds. The flagship race was now as much about feel as about scores.

## Google launches Gemini 3 (November 18)

Google answered with Gemini 3, its most intelligent model yet: state-of-the-art reasoning and multimodal understanding, a new high of 1501 Elo on the LMArena leaderboard, and a Deep Think mode for the hardest problems. It launched everywhere at once - in Search's AI Mode, the Gemini app, and Antigravity, a new agentic development environment where agents act across editor, terminal and browser.

## Anthropic raises the bar with Claude Opus 4.5 (November 24)

Anthropic closed the sprint with Claude Opus 4.5, its best model for coding, agents and computer use. It posted 80.9% on SWE-bench Verified, added an effort parameter that lets developers trade capability for cost, and landed at $5/$25 per million tokens - a third of its predecessor's price. Twelve days, three flagships, no pause.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

Three frontier launches in twelve days means capability is no longer the constraint - integration is. Each model reads, reasons and acts over live data, and each fails the same way when the data is a mess. Structured, validated extraction is what turns a frontier model into a working system. That is precisely what NextRows is for.
