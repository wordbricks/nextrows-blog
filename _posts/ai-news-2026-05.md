One week in May compressed a year of industry news - and the rest of the month kept pace.

## I/O 2026: Search becomes an agent runtime (May 19)

At Google I/O, the company launched the Gemini 3.5 family "built to help you reliably execute complex, multi-step agentic workflows," and rebuilt its flagship product around agents. Information agents came to Search: background agents that "monitor information on your behalf and send detailed updates with links," running 24/7. The Gemini app gained Gemini Spark, a proactive assistant that manages inboxes and schedules in the background. And the search box itself got what Google called its biggest upgrade in over 25 years. Google did not add an agent to Search; it turned Search into one.

## Anthropic passes OpenAI in business adoption (May 13 and 20)

Ramp's AI Index showed Anthropic passing OpenAI in business adoption for the first time - 34.4% of businesses versus 32.3%, after Anthropic quadrupled adoption in a year. A week later, CNBC confirmed Anthropic was on track for $10.9 billion in Q2 revenue, which a source said would deliver the company's first profitable quarter. The enterprise race had a new leader by at least one hard metric: where businesses actually spend.

## OpenAI's IPO plans surface (May 20)

Reuters and CNBC reported that OpenAI was preparing to file confidentially for an IPO, possibly within days - the first concrete step toward the most anticipated listing in tech history. (The confidential filing itself was reported on June 8.) A public OpenAI would answer to quarterly earnings, with consequences for the API pricing every builder depends on.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

When the world's biggest front door to the web becomes an agent that monitors pages around the clock, "keep an eye on this site" stops being a human chore. Structured, validated web data - the thing NextRows produces - becomes the input these agents actually consume.
