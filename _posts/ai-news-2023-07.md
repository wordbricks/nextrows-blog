July rearranged the economics of AI in two directions at once: news archives became licensed training material, and a frontier-class open model became free for businesses to build on.

## Anthropic releases Claude 2 (July 11)

Anthropic opened Claude 2 to the public in the US and UK, with a 100,000-token context window - enough for entire books or codebases in one prompt - and improved scores on coding, math and writing evaluations. It was the clearest challenge yet to ChatGPT from another frontier lab, built around Anthropic's emphasis on safety techniques like Constitutional AI.

## The AP licenses its archive to OpenAI (July 13)

The Associated Press agreed to license part of its news archive, reaching back to 1985, to OpenAI under a two-year agreement that also gave the AP access to OpenAI's technology. It was the first major deal of its kind between a news organization and an AI company, and a template - after months of threats - for paying for training data instead of litigating it.

## Meta and Microsoft launch Llama 2 (July 18)

Meta released Llama 2, ranging from 7 to 70 billion parameters, free for research and commercial use, with Microsoft as preferred partner distributing it through Azure and Windows. A restriction for services above 700 million monthly users kept the largest platforms out, but for everyone else, a strong foundation model stopped being a research courtesy and became a starting line.

## Why this month matters if you work with web data

July opened the model economy: open weights for builders, licensed archives for trainers. Either way, the raw material is web-scale data turned into usable structure, and that conversion is what NextRows automates from a plain-language description.
